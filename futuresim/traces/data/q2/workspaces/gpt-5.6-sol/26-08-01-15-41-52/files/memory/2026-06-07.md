## Calibration lessons

- Sparse named-entity questions need geographically and institutionally broader candidate generation. Oman, Air Canada, and Wilton Sampaio were complete-set misses, so the loss came from candidate coverage rather than fine probability calibration.
- Do not map a commercial mechanism mechanically to the commerce secretary. An international promotion agreement can fall primarily within the secretary of state's diplomatic remit; Rubio deserved a larger prior tail than his 0.25.
- When direct evidence creates a clear plurality, retaining meaningful concentration is rewarded. Platner, Oyarzabal, and Mindanao all validated evidence-led leaders; flattening these forecasts would have reduced skill.
- Preserve residual mass when only five candidates can be named, but use the slots to cover distinct regions, organizations, and role pathways instead of tightly correlated alternatives.
