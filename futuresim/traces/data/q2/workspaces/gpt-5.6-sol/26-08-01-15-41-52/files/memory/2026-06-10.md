## Calibration lessons

- Coalition answers require both broad country coverage and exact source formatting. The peace-fund answer used `UK, Australia, Canada`; forecasts omitted Australia and used `United Kingdom` plus conjunction-style formatting.
- A squad replacement fills a roster slot, not necessarily the injured player's tactical role or leadership role. Shuto Machino's selection shows that replacement pools should span positions rather than cluster around like-for-like midfield or captaincy substitutes.
- Linked contracts can impose strong logical constraints. The US double-scorer and final-goal questions describe the same Paraguay match, so their answers should share a distribution. The later Senegal record contract says the repeat-World-Cup scorer is still unknown after the France match, favoring players who had not scored at an earlier World Cup for Senegal's first goal against France.
- Preserve candidate coverage even when a competitor looks likelier to win than finish in a specified lower position. Kimi Antonelli's omission from an exact-third Formula One pool was unjustified after five straight victories.
- Low-base-rate tournaments such as golf majors need calibrated absolute win probabilities, not normalized top-five shares. Leaving residual mass is essential.
